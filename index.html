ABOUT

      

        
> I found my way into tech through building, not through a classroom.
           Long before computer science was a subject I studied formally, it was
           just the thing I did after school.


        
> It started young — poking around ethical hacking basics around age
           9-10, out of pure curiosity about how systems actually worked underneath.
           Around the same time I was building RC cars out of scrap motors, well
           before I understood what a circuit even was. At 13 I built a 2D game in
           Unity, mostly by breaking things and figuring out why they broke.


        
> Along the way I wrote two books that had nothing to do with code — a
           poetry collection, and a long-form essay analyzing gaps in India's
           education system. Different medium, same instinct: notice a problem,
           sit with it, and produce something real about it.


        
> Right now I'm on a self-directed gap year. No institutional
           affiliation, no assigned curriculum — I set my own scope and ship
           against it. That's meant competing in 24+ hackathons, including a solo
           finish in the top 12 of 40,000+ entrants against university teams.


        
> I also serve as Country Director, India for Recycle My Battery
           (RMB), an international NGO — running school outreach and building
           partnerships to get e-waste recycling programs into classrooms.


        
> Across all of it, one rule hasn't changed:
           a project only counts if it actually runs.
           Not planned. Not pitched. Running, in front of real users.
